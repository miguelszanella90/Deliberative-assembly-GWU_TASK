Game Handbook

Seven rounds · Ten participants · Three scoreboards · Fictional policy scenarios

Research question · Continuous Inductive Learning

Research question: What is the effect of continuous inductive learning on collective reasoning and decision-making among randomly assigned participants in controlled, scheduled deliberative sessions, where reasoning capacities, communication opportunities, and power relationships vary under conditions of time pressure and uncertainty?

Continuous Inductive Learning denotes the ongoing presentation, exchange, evaluation, and potential use of relevant inductive information during repeated deliberative sessions. It does not mean judgments must improve monotonically in every round.

The proposed causal comparison is between randomly assigned independent assemblies in a low-information control and a high-information treatment; this interactive assisted pilot alternates conditions for demonstration and records behavioral choices.

1 · Objective and competition
  • Personal: Compete for the highest individual score. A legislator's displayed total combines personal and presidential points.
  • Party: Help Blue or Red earn points through sponsored legislation and bipartisan passage.
  • President: Earn presidential points by passing bills and assembling bipartisan support.
  • Duration: The game ends after seven rounds. Scores are cumulative; no automatic early-victory trigger is implemented.
  • Other players: You control P1; P2–P10 are simulated and make probabilistic decisions.
2 · Game setup and institutional roles
  • Parties: Five Blue and five Red seats; affiliation stays fixed for the game.
  • Incentives: Each player is assigned a fixed Collective, Party, or Individual motivation.
  • President: A randomly ordered rotation assigns a different President for each of the seven rounds. The President chooses the final policy word and may issue a directive or call a briefing.
  • Caucus leaders: One eligible Blue and one eligible Red player are randomly appointed each round. They can direct their caucus and call for review.
  • Institutional Expert: One non-President is selected each round, favoring players who have served fewer times. Experts do not vote. P1 may act as Expert at most once per game.
  • Bill sponsor: Blue, Red, or Joint; the seven-round sequence contains two of each and one extra randomized sponsor.
  • Institutional friction: Caucus Pressure, Expert Challenge, Presidential Discretion, or Low Friction is randomized by round.
3 · Round sequence
  1. Initial word: Choose one of three proposed policy words and record confidence. Other players choose independently.
  2. Private information: If assigned a private card, review its fictional source (Executive, Judicial, Comparative Policy, University behavioral research, or Legislative evaluation), and explicitly choose to reveal or withhold. Participants without a card skip disclosure.
  3. Deliberation: Choose an argument and target one legislator using evidence, compromise, or coalition-building. Persuasion can succeed or fail.
  4. Revised word: Review revealed information and any external evidence; keep or change your preferred word and confidence.
  5. Presidential selection: The President selects the bill's final word. The assembly's word preferences and final legislative wording are not the same thing.
  6. Institutional briefing: After the President selects the complete bill wording, leaders may request a briefing before the vote. Players may attend at no attendance cost or decline for −2 personal points. The action is recorded, and declining does not prevent voting.
  7. Consequence preview and vote: Compare simulated YES/NO outcomes and expected scoring before committing. Nine eligible legislators can vote; five YES votes are required to pass.
  8. Feedback and learning reflection: Review outcomes; select one or more lessons with an optional note. Midway through the next round, after deliberation and before revising the word, report whether you applied a lesson from the previous round. These self-reports are not proof of actual learning.

Corrections: Back One Stage can reopen a previous decision before the next round. Individual votes remain hidden until the result stage.

4 · Personal scoring
  • Evidence alignment: +4 when your revised word matches the fictional evidence-supported option. Experts can earn these points too.
  • Legislative outcome: +3 if your YES/NO vote matches whether the bill passed or failed.
  • Policy outcome: +4 for voting YES on an evidence-aligned bill that passes, or NO on a non-aligned bill that fails.
  • Bipartisan cooperation: +2 for voting YES when the bill passes with support from both parties.
  • Sponsor-team success: +2 for voting YES on a passed bill sponsored by your own party or jointly sponsored. A NO vote on a failed bill does not earn this bonus.
  • Caucus loyalty: During Caucus Pressure, non-leaders gain +2 for following their party's directive or lose 4 for defying it.
  • Presidential loyalty: During Presidential Discretion, non-Presidents gain +2 for following the President's directive or lose 3 for defying it.
  • Expert: Does not cast YES/NO votes, so earns no vote-based or directive points; can still earn evidence-alignment points.
  • Personal total: Can fall below zero. Deliberation attempts do not currently award separate persuasion points.
5 · Party and presidential scoring
  • Blue or Red sponsorship: +10 party points to that sponsoring party if its bill passes.
  • Joint sponsorship: +5 party points to each party if the bill passes.
  • Bipartisan passage: +10 additional party points to each party when at least one YES vote comes from both parties.
  • President — passage: +10 presidential points when the bill passes.
  • President — bipartisanship: +10 more presidential points for bipartisan passage.
  • Failed bill: No sponsoring-party or presidential passage bonus.
6 · Evidence, pressure, and vote simulation
  • Information: Evidence is randomized within the game-assigned control or treatment condition. Private cards come from five fictional source families: Executive assessments, decided Judicial cases, Comparative Policy studies, University behavioral experiments, and Legislative impact evaluations. Cards vary in relevance and may support, challenge, or complicate the proposed policy. Disclosing a private card remains optional.
  • Two seven-round experimental conditions: Experiment 1 — Low-information control has six low-information rounds (no added external evidence) and one randomly placed medium-information round (one additional statement). Experiment 2 — High-information treatment has six high-information rounds (two statements) and one randomly placed medium-information round. The rare medium round is retained in each condition. Later games alternate low/high by experiment number. Policy topics remain identical in their seven-case pool; their order and institutional roles are randomized. The condition affects evidence availability, not whether players must accept or share it.
  • Private evidence: The low-information control distributes only two private evidence cards per participant across seven rounds; the high-information treatment distributes five. Source families are randomized and evidence can be withheld. This makes availability different at both institutional and personal levels.
  • Conditions: Communication (Open/Restricted), time (Normal/Short), and presidential authority (Consultative/Strong) vary randomly.
  • Institutional review: A requested caucus or presidential briefing pauses voting until you attend and finish it or decline it for −2 personal points. YES/NO requests are shown only with the final bill wording at Step 6.
  • Vote forecast: The YES/NO panel estimates passage chances and conditional point consequences from the bot voting model. It does not reveal actual votes or guarantee the displayed result.
  • Policy benchmark: A pre-coded word is used to approximate decision quality in each fictional scenario; it is not a judgment about real-world policy.
7 · Table, records, and exports
  • Assembly table: See each player’s institutional role, initial and revised words, changes, whether their private information was shared, and the source of publicly shared cards. Votes appear when voting closes.
  • Decision trail: The event log records submitted judgments, sharing, argument and persuasion choices, leadership directives, revisions, votes, and point changes.
  • Scores: Personal, presidential, and party totals accumulate across rounds. Individual totals are shown after each round.
  • Exports: Download round summaries, player-round records, event-level logs, or the combined research dataset. Use View / Copy Data if the preview blocks downloads.
  • Corrections: Back One Stage replaces subsequent simulated choices from the reverted stage; it does not preserve discarded branches as observations.
8 · Research limitations
  • Simulation: The nine automated legislators help test how a proposed randomized control/treatment design operates. In the future research experiment, use the same assignments and behavioral measures with the intended participants.
  • Inference: Conditions are randomized, but seven linked rounds vary in policy content, sequence, and learning history; within-game contrasts are descriptive.
  • Behavior: Changing one's mind is not automatically good reasoning. Maintaining an evidence-supported position can also be appropriate.
  • Future human testing: Would need separate participant consent and data procedures.
